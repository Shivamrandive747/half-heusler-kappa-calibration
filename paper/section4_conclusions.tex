\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Published first-principles calculations overestimate the measured lattice thermal conductivity of
half Heuslers by a median factor of \num{1.69}, in \num{10} of \num{12} bonding families, and the
offset shrinks as temperature rises. Defect and boundary scattering added to intrinsic phonon
scattering would give such a trend, and so would an electronic contribution left in the measured
values; the data do not separate them (Section~\ref{sec:interpretation}). Because the offset is systematic,
a two-parameter transfer function corrects it. For compounds with a measured family-mate,
refitting its magnitude within the bonding family lowers the median held-out error from
\SI{32.3}{\percent} to \SI{21.7}{\percent}, although it is the better of the two for only
\num{16} of the \num{27} compounds on which they differ (sign test $p=\num{0.44}$). The
two-parameter correction also carries over to the
calculations of a published machine-learned force field, whose median error against measurement it
lowers from \SI{101.4}{\percent} to \SI{36.8}{\percent}.

Combined with a machine-learning model that predicts the calculated value from crystal structure,
the correction was tested against measurement with each compound's chemistry cluster held out of
every fitted step. Within the domain of applicability the median error is \SI{33.1}{\percent} and
\SI{87.2}{\percent} of \num{39} compounds lie within a factor of two (\SI{37.4}{\percent} over all \num{50} measured compounds). A constant fitted in the same way,
with no information about the compound, has a median error of \SI{35.9}{\percent}, which is not
significantly worse. Inside the domain the typical disagreement with measurement, a
factor of \num{1.35},
is similar in size to the \num{1.36} by which two laboratories measuring the same compound differ,
so further gains are likely to depend on better reference measurements as much as on better
models.

The method turns calculated conductivities into estimates that can be compared directly with
experiment. We issue \num{10} predictions with intervals for unmeasured compounds, and give values
from crystal structure alone for \ce{TmPbAu} and \ce{HfTeOs}, which have neither a measurement
nor a published full calculation in our corpus. Within the domain of Section~\ref{sec:domain} and the limits of
Section~\ref{sec:limitations}, each issued prediction can be tested by a single synthesis and
measurement of the cubic phase.

The measurements that would most strengthen the method are a first measurement in families with no
measured member, such as \ce{Pb}--\ce{Au} and \ce{Te}--\ce{Os}; independent bulk measurements in
families that rest on one compound or one paper; measurements over a wide temperature range on dense
specimens, to constrain the exponent; and two \ce{Ni}--\ce{Bi} measurements free of the bipolar
contribution, which would allow nine of the ten conditional estimates to be issued.
