\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We begin with the offset that the transfer function removes and test the function on published
calculations. We then assess the machine-learning model against published calculations, combine the
two steps against measurement, and close with the predictions the method issues and declines.

\subsection{Published calculations systematically overestimate measured $\kappa_L$}
\label{sec:offset}

A correction is possible only if the offset between calculation and experiment is systematic. We pair
every in-domain half Heusler that has both a published full calculation and a laboratory
measurement, each compound scored against its single reference laboratory: \num{34} compounds and
\num{429} calculation--measurement pairs, reduced for the figures to \num{189} points, one per
compound per \SI{100}{\kelvin} bin. The calculation exceeds the measurement by a median factor of
\num{1.69} (each compound's median ratio, then the median over compounds;
Fig.~\ref{fig:offset}(d)). The excess is systematic, appearing in
\num{29} of the \num{34} compounds and in \num{10} of the \num{12} bonding families. The five
exceptions, \ce{HfSnPt}, \ce{TiSnPt}, \ce{ZrSnPt}, \ce{ErBiPd} and \ce{LaBiPd}, all belong to the two
families whose median calculation lies below measurement, \ce{Sn}--\ce{Pt} and \ce{Bi}--\ce{Pd}. On
average the calculated-to-measured ratio falls towards one as temperature rises
(Fig.~\ref{fig:offset}(c)), consistent with extrinsic scattering truncating long mean free paths,
although the data do not identify a mechanism. Because the offset shrinks with temperature, it is
largest at room temperature: the fitted constant $c=\num{0.42}$ (Section~\ref{sec:transferresults})
places the calculations about $1/c \approx 2.4$ times above measurement at \SI{300}{\kelvin}, whereas
\num{1.69} is the median over all measured temperatures. Its possible sources are discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:interpretation}.

\subsection{A two-parameter transfer function removes the offset}
\label{sec:transferresults}

\paragraph{The shared constant} Fitted on the \num{34} compounds of Section~\ref{sec:offset}, the
shared form gives $c=\num{0.42}$ and $p=\num{0.70}$; the magnitude is stable and the exponent is
not (Section~\ref{sec:transfer}). Correcting each compound with the shared constant refitted without its own chemistry cluster moves
the median ratio from \num{1.69} to \num{0.99} and the fraction of compounds within a factor of two
of measurement from \SI{68}{\percent} to \SI{88}{\percent} (Fig.~\ref{fig:offset}(b) and (d)). The
cap of the shared form binds only above \SI{1036}{\kelvin}, where \num{3} of the \num{189} points
lie (Fig.~\ref{fig:offset}(c)).

\paragraph{One constant per bonding family}
\label{sec:familycal}
\label{sec:deployed}
A family \emph{member} is a compound with both a usable reference measurement and a
temperature-matched published calculation. Each of the \num{12} families with a member carries its
own constant (Eq.~\eqref{eq:family}). We test it leave-one-compound-out, because that mirrors how a
prediction is made: an unmeasured target is predicted from its measured family-mates. Inside each
fold the family-mates' references are re-chosen, and the shared exponent is refitted without the
held-out compound, so that the held-out compound cannot influence either.

Table~\ref{tab:famcal} and Fig.~\ref{fig:family}(a) give the families. Five families are validated
held out:
\ce{Sb}--\ce{Pt} (\SI{7.4}{\percent}), \ce{Ni}--\ce{Sb} (\SI{16.4}{\percent}), \ce{Fe}--\ce{Sb}
(\SI{17.3}{\percent}), \ce{Sn}--\ce{Pt} (\SI{21.7}{\percent}) and \ce{Co}--\ce{Sb}
(\SI{24.9}{\percent}). In \ce{Sn}--\ce{Pt} the family constant more than halves the shared
constant's error (\SI{50.4}{\percent}), and it is also clearly better in \ce{Sb}--\ce{Pt}
(\SI{30.4}{\percent} with the shared constant) and \ce{Fe}--\ce{Sb} (\SI{28.7}{\percent}). The
\ce{Sb}--\ce{Pt} result rests on thin data: three of its four members (\ce{DySbPt}, \ce{LuSbPt},
\ce{TbSbPt}) come from one paper at three temperatures each, below the four-temperature rule used
elsewhere, and enter the corpus only through language-model extraction, whose independent check
(Section~S3 of the Supplementary material) rests on four points. With three compounds in most
families, a family's status is sensitive to single compounds; \ce{Co}--\ce{Sb} passes narrowly. In
\ce{Ni}--\ce{Sb} and \ce{Co}--\ce{Sb} the shared
constant does as well or better on the same compounds (\SI{10.1}{\percent} and
\SI{23.0}{\percent}); the pre-specified rule still gives every family with measured members its own
constant, so the issued \ce{Ni}--\ce{Sb} values carry \num{0.47} (shared constant: \num{0.42}).

Each of the nine families with two or more members reproduces its own reference papers closely:
fitted in sample, the median error is \SIrange{2.4}{13.9}{\percent}, and the anchored families fit
\ce{ZrCoBi} (\ce{Co}--\ce{Bi}) to \SI{6.3}{\percent} and \ce{ZrNiPb} (\ce{Ni}--\ce{Pb}) to
\SI{5.6}{\percent}. In four families, \ce{Bi}--\ce{Pd}, \ce{Ni}--\ce{Sn}, \ce{Co}--\ce{Sn} and
\ce{Sb}--\ce{Pd}, the constant fits each paper (\SIrange{2.8}{7.3}{\percent}) but does not transfer
from one member to another within the \SI{25}{\percent} bar, because the members, as made, are
different materials: a nanostructured sample set against a dense one, or members measured in
different laboratories (discussed below). These four are reported as \emph{calibrated, not
validated}: the constant describes each member's own reference, but a held-out member is not
predicted within the bar. Their held-out results and the reason in each family are in Section~S6 of
the Supplementary material, and no prediction is issued through them.

\paragraph{Pooled over the held-out compounds} Thirty-one of the \num{34} compounds belong to a
family with at least one other measured member and can be predicted held out. On the \num{31} such
compounds the family constant gives a median error of \SI{21.7}{\percent}, with \SI{93.5}{\percent}
within a factor of two and \num{17} of the \num{31} under \SI{25}{\percent}. The shared constant,
refitted without each held-out compound and scored on the same \num{31}, gives \SI{32.3}{\percent},
with \SI{83.9}{\percent} within a factor of two. The family constant lowers the median but is not
significantly better compound by compound: it is the better of the two for \num{16} of the
\num{31}, worse for \num{11} and tied for \num{4} (two-sided sign test on the \num{27} untied,
$p=\num{0.44}$). A capped family form, applied throughout, gives \SI{27.0}{\percent}; the uncapped
form is preferred in all \num{31} folds. The same comparison over all \num{34} compounds, which mixes
in the three single-member fits, is given in Section~S7 of the Supplementary material.

Figure~\ref{fig:family}(c)--(e) shows held-out family fits on individual curves: the three lowest
held-out errors among the \num{13} compounds in validated families measured at five or more
temperatures spanning at least \SI{300}{\kelvin}, namely \ce{TiSnPt} (\SI{7.7}{\percent}), \ce{LuNiSb}
(\SI{8.0}{\percent}) and \ce{VFeSb} (\SI{11.4}{\percent}). These are the best cases; the
median over the \num{13} is \SI{18.0}{\percent}. Because the family form is uncapped, the
calibrated \ce{TiSnPt} curve rises above the calculation above about \SI{400}{\kelvin}, as the
measurements do. This follows the reference data and is not a claim that the lattice conductivity
exceeds that of the perfect crystal; such values more likely retain part of the electronic
contribution (Section~\ref{sec:interpretation}). Every issued prediction lies below the calculation
it calibrates.

\paragraph{The shared constant on families never measured}
\label{sec:unseenfamily}
For an unmeasured family only the shared constant applies. Withholding one family at a time from the
fit and predicting its members gives, over the \num{34} compounds, a median error of
\SI{33.0}{\percent} with \SI{79.4}{\percent} within a factor of two and \SI{32.4}{\percent} within
\SI{25}{\percent}; per-family medians range from \SI{12.8}{\percent} (\ce{Ni}--\ce{Sb}) to
\SI{116.8}{\percent} (\ce{Sb}--\ce{Pd}). Every shared-constant estimate in this paper carries this
calibration error.

\subsection{The regression model reproduces published calculations}
\label{sec:mlrepro}

Where no calculation is published, the model supplies one. Scored against the published full
calculations of the \num{230} $\mathrm{VEC}=18$ half Heuslers, each predicted with its X-site
chemistry cluster withheld from training (\num{27} clusters), the median error is
\SI{21.9}{\percent} (reference seed), with \SI{88.3}{\percent} within a factor of two and a median
ratio of \num{0.87} (Fig.~\ref{fig:modelblind}(a)). The result covers only the step from structure
to calculation. The end-to-end test below already includes this step, so the two errors should not
be compounded.

\subsection{The model's attributions are consistent with phonon physics}
\label{sec:shap}

We decompose the reference-seed model, fitted on all \num{681} training rows, with \textsc{shap}
(SHapley Additive exPlanations)~\cite{lundberg2017a}, which assigns each descriptor an additive
contribution to each prediction. Figure~\ref{fig:modelblind}(b) shows the eight descriptors with the
largest mean absolute contribution; Section~S4 of the Supplementary material gives the twelve
largest for every training row, with the size and direction of each. The full-Heusler flag leads: a
full Heusler is predicted to conduct less. Size and bond length come next: a larger X-site radius,
longer bonds, a larger sum of radii, more atoms per conventional cell and a heavier Y site all lower
the prediction, while a higher X-site electronegativity raises it, as does a lower temperature (the
ninth-ranked descriptor, $1000/T$). The
pattern is consistent with phonon physics: heavier atoms, longer and softer bonds and more atoms per
cell lower a lattice's conductivity, and Umklapp scattering weakens as temperature falls. Consistency
does not show that the model has learned that physics.

The attribution has three limits. It explains the model of the \emph{calculation}, not of a
measurement, which also depends on the specimen. Correlated descriptors share attribution (the sum
of radii and the mean radius, the shortest and mean bond lengths, atoms per cell and the
full-Heusler flag; correlation map in Section~S4 of the Supplementary material), so the ranking describes groups
of descriptors more reliably than individual ones. Finally, the full-Heusler flag partly reflects the
make-up of the pool, whose full-Heusler labels include semi-empirical estimates; it shifts all
half-Heusler predictions together and does not reorder them.

\subsection{From crystal structure to measurement: the held-out test against experiment}
\label{sec:blind}

The central test of the paper asks how close the full route, model plus shared transfer function,
comes to measurement for a compound whose chemistry was withheld from every fitted step.
We score the \num{39} in-domain half Heuslers, each against its single reference laboratory with its
X-site chemistry cluster removed from both the model's training and the fit of the shared constant.

Table~\ref{tab:accuracy} gathers the accuracy of every route against measurement. The calibrated
prediction has a median error of \SI{33.1}{\percent} (bootstrap 95\,\% interval
\SIrange{23.6}{38.9}{\percent}) and places \SI{87.2}{\percent} of compounds within a factor of two
of measurement (Fig.~\ref{fig:modelblind}(c) and (d)), both medians over five model seeds. Across
seeds the median error runs from \SI{24.6}{\percent} to \SI{35.4}{\percent}, so it is best read as
roughly one third; the within-$2\times$ fraction moves only from \SI{87.2}{\percent} to
\SI{89.7}{\percent}. The reference seed gives \SI{31.7}{\percent}, below the seed median, so results
quoted at that seed may slightly favour the model. The correction moves the median ratio of
predicted to measured conductivity from \num{1.40} to \num{0.90}. Being a multiplier, it cannot
remove the compression visible in Fig.~\ref{fig:modelblind}(c): the lowest-$\kappa_L$ compounds tend
to be over-predicted and the highest under-predicted.

\paragraph{Against compound-blind baselines}
\label{sec:vsnulls}
Both null baselines are refitted with the same chemistry held out and scored against the same
references. The model route is closer to measurement for \SI{71.8}{\percent} of compounds. Paired
compound by compound, it is better by a median \num{10.3} percentage points than the constant null
(95\,\% interval \numrange{3.8}{22.9}) and by \num{11.2} points than the power law
(\numrange{3.5}{22.9}). A paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test gives $p=\num{0.004}$ against each null
at the median over seeds, and $p=\num{0.007}$ after a Holm correction for the two comparisons; on
the least favourable seed the corrected value is \num{0.056}. Because compounds of one chemistry
cluster share a model fold and often a laboratory, we repeat the test with each of the \num{18}
clusters counted once. The Wilcoxon test then still gives $p<0.05$ on every seed (largest
\num{0.048}), but the sign test does not, so the cluster-level evidence is marginal. The nulls place
almost as many compounds within a factor of two (\SI{84.6}{\percent}): the gain lies in the typical
error rather than in the tail.

\paragraph{Without the domain screens} Over all \num{50} measured half Heuslers the same protocol
gives \SI{37.4}{\percent} (seed range \SIrange{33.6}{38.9}{\percent}), against
\SI{48.2}{\percent} for the uncorrected model (both medians over seeds). It still improves on both
nulls, more weakly (paired Wilcoxon $p=\num{0.035}$ and \num{0.033} at the reference seed;
Section~S7).

\subsection{Robustness and comparisons}
\label{sec:robust}

\paragraph{Further checks}
\label{sec:failure}
Three further checks support the result; Section~S7 gives each in full. The eleven compounds the
screens exclude behave as a separate population, with a median error of \SI{86.4}{\percent} against
\SI{31.7}{\percent} inside the domain at the reference seed (open squares in
Fig.~\ref{fig:modelblind}(c)). Dropping the temperature term
($\kappa_L^{\mathrm{exp}} = c\,\kappa_L^{\mathrm{BTE}}$) raises the median error to
\SI{38.5}{\percent} (median over seeds). Published semi-empirical estimates
(Slack~\cite{slack1979the} and Debye--Callaway~\cite{callaway1959model}) do far worse, at \SI{91.6}{\percent} on the \num{22}
in-domain compounds that have one. The predictions also order the compounds: Spearman's $\rho$
between predicted and measured conductivity is \num{0.662}.

\paragraph{Where the error falls, and a family-informed baseline}
\label{sec:familynull}
Counting the blind-test compounds of each family, which include measured compounds without a
published calculation, the worst families on the model route at the reference seed are
\ce{Co}--\ce{Sn} (\SI{59.4}{\percent}) and \ce{Ni}--\ce{Sn} (\SI{55.4}{\percent}), three compounds
each, and the best is \ce{Ni}--\ce{Sb} (\SI{13.7}{\percent}, eight compounds); the grouping is
descriptive, since the holdout unit is the X site. A stronger baseline fits a power law to the
measured $\kappa(T)$ of the held-out compound's own family, with no calculation. It is defined for
\num{34} of the \num{39} in-domain compounds (all but \ce{AlVNi}, \ce{YNiBi}, \ce{ZrCoBi},
\ce{ZrNiPb} and \ce{ZrSbIr}; not the same \num{34} as the published route). There it gives
\SI{25.4}{\percent} against \SI{31.6}{\percent} for the model route at the reference seed, and
neither is significantly better (paired Wilcoxon $p=\num{0.99}$). Where a family has been measured,
then, the model route does not beat this baseline on absolute accuracy; but the baseline cannot
order compounds within a family and says nothing where no member is measured. For scale,
calibrating a published calculation with the shared constant gives \SI{32.3}{\percent}, comparable
to the model route's \SI{33.1}{\percent}; the \SI{32.3}{\percent} is measured on the \num{31}
compounds of the family test under the weaker, leave-one-compound-out holdout.

\paragraph{An independent machine-learned force field}
\label{sec:sdnnff}

Elemental-SDNNFF obtains $\kappa_L$ through the Boltzmann transport equation on a neural-network
force field~\cite{rodriguez2023millionsca}; its values are tier~2 and were never labels (the same
paper's calculations from first-principles force constants are tier~1 and enter the published
median for \num{6} of the \num{18}
compounds below, so the two are not wholly independent). On the
\num{18} in-domain compounds with a published calculation it reproduces that calculation at
\SI{300}{\kelvin} to a median \SI{18.8}{\percent} (ratio \num{0.99}), yet on the \num{21} it shares
with our measured set it lies a median factor of \num{2.01} above measurement (\SI{101.4}{\percent}
median error). The offset therefore survives intact to the end of a screening pipeline. The shared
constant, refitted without each compound's chemistry cluster, brings the SDNNFF values to
\SI{36.8}{\percent} (paired Wilcoxon $p=\num{0.0033}$), so the correction transfers to a
separately computed set of calculations. Our model route gives \SI{44.7}{\percent} on the same \num{21}
(reference seed): closer to measurement than raw SDNNFF for \num{16} of them, though not as close as
calibrated SDNNFF, whose values are given no holdout. Section~S7 gives the overlap between the two
sets of calculations and the matching windows.

\subsection{Ten predictions for unmeasured compounds}
\label{sec:screening}
\label{sec:issued}

We apply the family route to half Heuslers that have a published transport calculation but no
measurement. A candidate is issued only if it meets every condition below:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Inside the domain of applicability} (Section~\ref{sec:domain}), with no radioactive
        and no valence-fluctuating element.
  \item \textbf{A bonding family whose constant has been checked}: \emph{validated} (held-out error
        under \SI{25}{\percent}) or \emph{anchored} on a single member whose in-sample fit is under
        the same bar, an anchored prediction carrying that label (Table~\ref{tab:famcal}). Passing
        the in-sample bar shows only that the transfer-function shape fits that member's curve, not
        that the constant transfers to another compound.
  \item \textbf{A full transport calculation}, independent calculations agreeing within a factor of
        two at the quoted temperature.
  \item \textbf{Inside, or only marginally outside, the family's measured range.} A value beyond the
        family's measured span by less than a factor of \num{1.33}, the median disagreement between
        laboratories, is issued with a \emph{marginal} label; a value further outside is flagged.
        An anchored family has a single member and so defines no span; this condition does not apply
        to it.
\end{enumerate}

Ten compounds meet every condition (Table~\ref{tab:predictions}, Fig.~\ref{fig:predictions}). Three
lie inside the span of a validated family: \ce{TmSbPt} in \ce{Sb}--\ce{Pt}, and \ce{GdNiSb} and
\ce{TbNiSb} in \ce{Ni}--\ce{Sb}, each differing from the measured members only on the rare-earth
site. Three are marginal extrapolations in \ce{Sb}--\ce{Pt}: \ce{ErSbPt} and \ce{HoSbPt} a factor of
\num{1.15} and \num{1.12} above the family's span, and \ce{GdSbPt} \num{1.03} below it. As a check,
the \num{6} measured compounds lying this close outside their family-mates' span were predicted held
out to a median of \SI{17.4}{\percent}, \num{3} of them under \SI{25}{\percent}, against
\SI{18.8}{\percent} for the \num{19} inside it (two-sided Mann--Whitney $p=\num{0.51}$). Four are
issued through anchored families: \ce{HfCoBi} and \ce{TiCoBi} through \ce{Co}--\ce{Bi}, and
\ce{HfNiPb} and \ce{TiNiPb} through \ce{Ni}--\ce{Pb}. Four of the ten are issued through
\ce{Sb}--\ce{Pt}, whose
validation rests mainly on one paper (Section~\ref{sec:familycal}). \ce{TiCoBi}, \ce{HfNiPb} and \ce{TiNiPb} are quoted at
\SI{500}{\kelvin}, the temperature of their only uncontested calculation (for \ce{TiNiPb}, the two
\SI{300}{\kelvin} calculations disagree \num{5.6}-fold).

\subsection{Flagged compounds and the measurements that would resolve them}
\label{sec:refusals}

Fifteen compounds carry a calibrated value but are declined (\emph{flagged}); Section~S11 of the
Supplementary material tabulates each with the check it fails and draws every status on one axis. Nine are
\ce{Bi}--\ce{Pd} compounds, a family calibrated on its papers but not validated held out; seven also
lie a factor of \numrange{1.36}{1.86} above the \SIrange{3.2}{3.5}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin} span
of the two \ce{Bi}--\ce{Pd} compounds measured near room temperature. \ce{TmSbPd} and \ce{DySbPd}
belong to \ce{Sb}--\ce{Pd}, likewise calibrated but not validated, and \ce{DySbPd} is also
polymorphic. \ce{LaSbPt} and \ce{ScSbPt} are clear extrapolations in a validated family:
\ce{LaSbPt} lies a factor of \num{2.00} below the measured \ce{Sb}--\ce{Pt} floor and \ce{ScSbPt}
\num{1.97} above its ceiling. \ce{HfSbIr}
and \ce{TiSbIr} belong to \ce{Sb}--\ce{Ir}, whose single measured member its own constant does not
reproduce within the bar. The range criterion is conservative and unvalidated: the two held-out compounds that lay further outside their
family's span were predicted to a median of \SI{18.4}{\percent}, but two cases cannot justify issuing
clear extrapolations.

Ten further estimates, in \ce{Ni}--\ce{Bi}, are conditional. The family's only measured member,
\ce{YNiBi}, has a thermal conductivity with a significant bipolar contribution, as its authors
report~\cite{li2015synthesis}, and the lattice term we extract from it rises with temperature, so
the family cannot be calibrated. On the shared
constant the ten lie at \SIrange{2.21}{4.95}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin} at \SI{300}{\kelvin};
Section~S12 of the Supplementary material lists them with the two measurements that would allow
nine of them to be issued. One of the ten, \ce{SmNiBi}, also carries a caveat, because samarium's valence can vary
between compounds; it is counted as conditional, not among the fifteen flagged. Across the three statuses the
conformal band has the same relative width; what changes is the evidence behind the value, not its
stated precision.

For one family a measurement of the cubic phase may not be possible at all. The cobalt arsenides
\ce{XCoAs} (X = Ti, Zr, Hf) have published calculations but no measured family member, and the two
we found reported as synthesised, \ce{ZrCoAs} and \ce{HfCoAs}, crystallise in the orthorhombic
\ce{Co2Si} type~\cite{kleinke1998crystal}. We therefore carry none of the three as a candidate, so none has a
status. The polymorph screen alone would have caught only \ce{HfCoAs}, which has an orthorhombic
record (space group 62), and not \ce{ZrCoAs} or \ce{TiCoAs}, whose records carry only the cubic
cell.

\subsection{Two predictions from crystal structure alone}
\label{sec:structonly}

For compounds with no published lattice-thermal-conductivity calculation, the model supplies
$\kappa_L^{\mathrm{BTE}}$ and the shared constant maps it onto the experimental scale. Each value
carries two separately validated errors, stated side by side: \SI{21.9}{\percent} for the model
step (reference seed; Section~\ref{sec:mlrepro}) and \SI{33.0}{\percent} for the shared constant on a family never
measured (Section~\ref{sec:unseenfamily}). No conformal band is given, because its coverage is not
validated for a family never measured. Two compounds are graded strong on the evidence for their
model step (for \ce{HfTeOs}, on a single family member); Fig.~\ref{fig:predictions} (bottom) gives
both values for each.

For \ce{TmPbAu} (\ce{Pb}--\ce{Au} family) we use the OQMD cubic cell of the lowest-energy ordering,
$a=\SI{6.782}{\angstrom}$. The six \ce{Pb}--\ce{Au} half Heuslers with published calculations are reproduced held out
to between \SI{1}{\percent} and \SI{13}{\percent} (median \SI{8.6}{\percent}), and the seeds agree
within a factor of \num{1.11}. The model gives \SI{3.31}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin} and the shared
constant ($c=\num{0.42}$) \SI{1.39}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin} at \SI{300}{\kelvin}. We found no
published report of \ce{TmPbAu}; a family neighbour, \ce{TbPbAu}, has been grown as single crystals in
the cubic half-Heusler structure~\cite{agarwal2026thermodyna}.

For \ce{HfTeOs} (\ce{Te}--\ce{Os} family) we use the AFLOW cubic cell of the lowest-energy ordering,
$a=\SI{6.33}{\angstrom}$. The strong grade rests on one member, \ce{TiTeOs}, reproduced to \SI{0.5}{\percent} held
out; the seeds spread by a factor of \num{1.23}. The model gives \SI{12.99}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}
and the shared constant \SI{5.46}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}. As a cross-check, the SDNNFF value
for its chemical twin \ce{ZrTeOs}~\cite{rodriguez2023millionsca}, carried to hafnium by the twin
ratio of the Extended Methods, gives \SI{14.06}{\watt\per\meter\per\kelvin}; the model value is
\num{0.92} times this. The twin ratio was validated on calculations (median error
\SI{12.2}{\percent}), not on a machine-learned input, so the check is weaker than that figure
suggests. We found no published report of \ce{HfTeOs}.

The search also found further compounds with no published $\kappa_L$ and a group with only a
machine-learned value from a published screen; Section~S16 of the Supplementary material reports
them, together with two compounds in calibrated families that are flagged.
