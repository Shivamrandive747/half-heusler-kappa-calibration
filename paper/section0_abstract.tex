\begin{abstract}
High-throughput searches for half-Heusler thermoelectrics increasingly rely on lattice thermal
conductivity ($\kappa_L$) from first-principles calculations, or from machine-learning models trained
on them. These calculations describe an ideal single crystal, whereas measurements are made on
polycrystalline samples, in which grain boundaries and defects scatter heat as well. The resulting gap
between calculated and measured $\kappa_L$ has been noted for individual compounds but not, to our
knowledge, quantified across a materials class, so the values used for screening carry an unknown, systematic bias. Here we
quantify and correct this bias for cubic half-Heusler compounds with 18 valence electrons. Using a curated literature corpus that
keeps calculated and measured values apart, we show that calculations overestimate measured $\kappa_L$
in most bonding families, and that the excess shrinks with temperature. Scattering by defects, added
to the intrinsic scattering between lattice vibrations, would give such a trend, but the data do not
single out one cause. A simple transfer function with this temperature dependence removes the median
offset, and refitting its magnitude within a bonding family lowers the median error further, though not
consistently compound by compound. Combined with a machine-learning model that predicts the calculated
value from crystal structure, and tested on chemistries withheld from all fitting, the method predicts
measured $\kappa_L$ in this class about as closely as independent laboratories agree with one another, and provides testable predictions
for compounds not yet measured.
\end{abstract}
